In modern football the defensive midfielder is three jobs in one: the architect of the transition, the safety net in front of the back four, and the heartbeat of the attack. At Euro 2024 two players defined the role on the way to the final — Spain's Rodri and England's Declan Rice.

Both are world-class. But their underlying data tells two very different stories about what a "midfield general" actually does. By looking at their touches, their progressions and their defensive output, we can see exactly how each one shaped his team's route to the final.

## 1. The territory: where they command

Start with where each player lives on the pitch. The touch maps show a clear difference in positional discipline.

<figure class="chart-figure">
  <img src="/charts/football/rodri_touches.png" alt="Rodri touch map at Euro 2024, concentrated in the central midfield">
  <figcaption>Rodri — the central hub. His heaviest concentration of touches sits in the left-centre of midfield, with high density right across the middle third.
  <span class="chart-source">StatsBomb event data, Euro 2024.</span></figcaption>
</figure>

Rodri is the ultimate central pivot — the point around which the Spanish machine rotates. His touches cluster in the middle of the park and stay there. Rice, by contrast, covers more ground vertically.

<figure class="chart-figure">
  <img src="/charts/football/rice_touches.png" alt="Declan Rice touch map at Euro 2024, spread deeper into the defensive third">
  <figcaption>Rice — more spread, and deeper. A good share of his touches come in the defensive third, where he dropped in to help an England build-up that often struggled for fluidity.
  <span class="chart-source">StatsBomb event data, Euro 2024.</span></figcaption>
</figure>

Rice's map pulls noticeably further back, into the defensive third. That isn't a criticism — it's a reflection of a role that asked him to drop deep and get England moving when the build-up stalled.

## 2. Passing vs carrying: the art of progression

The most revealing question for a deep midfielder is *how* he moves the ball forward. Here the two could hardly be more different.

<figure class="chart-figure">
  <img src="/charts/football/rodri_progressions.png" alt="Rodri progression map showing long diagonal passes into wide areas">
  <figcaption>Rodri the passer: long, incisive diagonals that spray the ball into wide areas to release Spain's wingers.
  <span class="chart-source">StatsBomb event data, Euro 2024.</span></figcaption>
</figure>

Rodri progresses with his passing — long diagonals, often targeting the right flank to unleash Spain's wide threats. A **94% completion rate from 436 passes** is remarkable given how many of those were genuinely progressive attempts. He doesn't just keep the ball; he keeps it while advancing it.

<figure class="chart-figure">
  <img src="/charts/football/rice_progressions.png" alt="Declan Rice progression map showing a high volume of ball carries">
  <figcaption>Rice the carrier: a far higher share of progressions come from driving with the ball himself.
  <span class="chart-source">StatsBomb event data, Euro 2024.</span></figcaption>
</figure>

Rice passes well too, but his progression map is defined by carries. He uses his physicality to drive through the middle of the pitch. With **690 minutes to Rodri's 521**, he was England's primary workhorse — the man most often asked to carry the ball out of pressure himself.

The defensive sides of their games split along the same line. Rice is the ball-winner — he excels in recoveries and duels, the player you want in a scrap, winning the ball back through physical intervention. Rodri is the game-reader — his defending is built on intelligence, ranking higher in blocks and clearances, cutting out danger before it ever requires a tackle.

## 4. Efficiency vs volume

Rice leads on raw volume, playing every single minute of every game — a testament to his stamina and to how much England needed him. But Rodri's efficiency is superior: more carries, ball receipts and passes *per 90 minutes*, at higher accuracy.

So the data points to a clean distinction. Rice is the more dynamic presence — covering more ground, winning more physical duels, carrying more. Rodri is the more controlling one — the man every Spanish pattern runs through.

Two honest limits before the verdict. First, final-third influence and progression are only one lens on a deep midfielder; they say nothing about off-ball positioning or the less glamorous screening work. Second, with different minutes, raw totals describe volume and the per-90 figures describe rate — read each accordingly. Within those limits, the contrast is real and it's measurable.

Rice is the shield. Rodri is the metronome.
