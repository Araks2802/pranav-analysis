Some chases are tense. This one was a statement. West Indies had made a perfectly respectable 171 in Lucknow, the kind of total that usually guarantees a contest. India knocked it off in **14.4 overs**, with eight wickets and more than five overs to spare, because Shreyas Iyer decided the game didn't need to last twenty.

## 171 that never looked like enough

West Indies' innings had a familiar shape: a bright start, a middle-overs stutter, and a finish that didn't quite kick. Sherfane Rutherford's 56 and Shai Hope's 52 did the heavy lifting, but the innings leaked wickets throughout — they lost all ten, folding on the final ball of the 20th over.

India lost Abhishek and Samson early, which on another day invites pressure. Instead it simply cleared the stage for Shreyas Iyer. His **102 off 42** is the sort of innings that reframes a target: once he was set, 171 stopped being a score to chase and became a number to embarrass. Ishan Kishan's unbeaten 37 was the quiet passenger on a very fast car.

## The takeaway

For West Indies, the lesson was blunt: 171 is below par when the opposition has a batter in this mood. Their innings wasn't bad — it was simply answered by one of the best T20I hundreds you'll see. India 1-0, and the tone for a wild series was set.
