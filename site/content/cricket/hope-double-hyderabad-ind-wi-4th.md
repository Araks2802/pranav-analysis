Whenever Shai Hope scored a hundred in this series, West Indies won. In Hyderabad he did it for the second time, and the pattern held. His 102 anchored a total of 247, India's reply stalled at 223 all out, and a series India had led 2-1 was suddenly level at 2-2 with everything riding on a decider.

## Hope builds a monster

West Indies batted first and, with Hope in this mood, built relentlessly. His **102 off 49** was the spine; Sherfane Rutherford (49 off 26) and Shimron Hetmyer (33 off 12) provided the acceleration around him.

India's 223 would win plenty of games, but not when you're chasing 248 and keep losing wickets at the wrong moments. Sanju Samson's 56 off 22 gave them a flying start, and Tilak Varma's 43 kept them in the hunt, but the regular loss of partners meant the required rate crept away. Being bowled out with three balls left tells the story: India threw everything at it and came up 24 short.

## The takeaway

Shai Hope was the story of the series, and this was his masterpiece — a second hundred that forced a decider. That decider, in Bengaluru, never got a proper hearing: rain washed it out with India 118 for 5 in a shortened game, and the series finished level at 2-2. A fair result for a tour West Indies made genuinely competitive.
