After the run-glut of Ranchi, the third T20I in Indore was the game where someone finally defended a total. India posted 223, West Indies fell 14 short at 209 for 7, and the series swung back India's way at 2-1. It was set up at the top by Abhishek Sharma and finished by a bowling effort that — for once in this series — actually held.

## A fast start, then a stumble

India's 223 was front-loaded. Abhishek Sharma's **69 off 30** blazed a powerplay worth 87, and for a while 250-plus looked on.

They didn't quite kick on — losing wickets steadily and being bowled out for 223 off the last ball rather than launching to 240-plus. On the evidence of this series, leaving 15-20 runs out there is usually fatal. This time it wasn't, which tells you something changed with the ball.

The difference from Ranchi was the powerplay. In the chase of 250, West Indies came out swinging; here they managed just 54 for 1 in the first six, and against a target of 224 that's a hole you rarely climb out of. Shimron Hetmyer (43) and Kyle Pooran (42) kept them in touch, but the required rate stayed stubbornly ahead and India's bowlers — leaking so badly two nights earlier — found enough control at the back end to close the door.

## The takeaway

Not India's best batting night, but their best bowling one — and in a series this high-scoring, that was worth more. 2-1 up, one win from the series, with everything still to play for.
