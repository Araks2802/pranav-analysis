If the series opener was India flexing, the second game was West Indies answering with interest. India piled up **249 for 5** — the highest total anyone managed across the five matches — and lost. West Indies chased it down with nine balls to spare, and the man at the centre of it was Shai Hope.

## India's 249 should have been plenty

There was nothing wrong with India's innings. Sanju Samson's 82 off 40, Tilak Varma's 44 off 17 and Ishan Kishan's 43 off 22 powered a total that clears 12 an over.

A target of 250 in a T20 is supposed to be close to unbeatable. The required rate is 12.5 an over for the full twenty — no margin for a quiet period anywhere. West Indies didn't have one.

Shai Hope's **102 off 45** was the definition of a controlled chase — he never let the required rate balloon, and once Rutherford joined him the game tilted fast. Their partnership did what you have to do to chase 250: keep the rate honest from ball one so the death overs become a finish, not a rescue. West Indies' 18.6 an over at the back end sealed it with overs to spare.

## The takeaway

India will feel they left runs out there and still can't understand how 249 wasn't enough. The honest answer: it almost always is, but Shai Hope had other ideas. Series level at 1-1, and West Indies had served notice this wasn't a one-sided tour.
