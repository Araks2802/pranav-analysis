Chasing 181 with ten wickets in hand should be a stroll. India found a way to make it a scrap. Two nights after their record chase in the series opener, they were coasting one moment and five down for 70 the next — before Tilak Varma produced the innings that decided the match and sealed a 2-0 series lead.

## A controllable total

New Zealand's 180 for 5 was built sensibly rather than spectacularly. Glenn Phillips (70 off 42) and Bevon Jacobs (58 off 46) put together the backbone, and the Black Caps did their best work at the death — lifting to 13 an over in the last five after a middling middle phase.

A target of 181 on this ground, after India had knocked off 226 two days earlier, looked like a formality. The powerplay said otherwise: India crawled to 44 for 1, scoring slower than they had while chasing a far bigger total. Something was off from the start.

## The collapse, then the calm

Then it nearly fell apart. India slid from a watchful start to **70 for 5** inside eleven overs — Sooryavanshi, Samson, Abhishek, Iyer and Axar all back, a top order undone by a Mitchell Santner masterclass.

Tilak Varma was the answer. His **51 off 25** was the coolest head in the building — he absorbed the collapse without panicking, picked his moments, and then accelerated exactly when the required rate demanded it. He deserves the headline, but the supporting act mattered too: **Naman Dhir's 31 off 17** was a genuinely important cameo, three sixes that turned a tense finish back in India's favour, and Nitish Kumar Reddy's 26 off 13 kept the tempo up alongside him. Those two lower-order hands are the difference between a nervy loss and a series-sealing win.

## Santner's lonely brilliance

The honest footnote for New Zealand is that they had the game won and let it slip — but not through poor bowling across the board.

Santner's 4 for 21 in four overs is the kind of spell that usually wins a T20. It didn't, because Jacob Duffy, Lockie Ferguson and Nathan Smith all went at better than ten an over around him, and India's lower order feasted on the overs that weren't Santner's. Defend 180 and you need more than one bowler to land. New Zealand had exactly one — and Tilak Varma made sure it wasn't enough.
